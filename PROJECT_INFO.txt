1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & OVERVIEW
================================================================================
Airiva (formerly known as APIx) is an automated economic statistics and price 
measurement platform designed to compute a real-time, high-frequency Airfare 
Price Index for the Indian domestic aviation market.

Unlike standard consumer goods whose prices remain sticky over days or weeks, 
airline seat fares are notoriously dynamic. Commercial airlines utilize automated 
algorithmic yield-management engines that recalculate passenger seat prices 
every few minutes based on booking horizon, historical load factors, competitor 
actions, and real-time flight capacity. As a consequence, measuring true airfare 
inflation in India has historically been fraught with statistical distortions, 
arbitrary sampling errors, and lack of transparency.

Airiva addresses this macroeconomic void by constructing a Consumer Price Index 
(CPI)-style superlative price index modelled on the highest international 
standards (analogous to the US Bureau of Transportation Statistics Air-Travel 
Price Index and Bureau of Labor Statistics consumer metrics).

Key Platform Capabilities:
  1. High-Frequency Multi-Source Ingestion:
     Automated daily scraping across India's largest scheduled commercial 
     carriers (IndiGo, Air India, Akasa Air, SpiceJet) and leading Online Travel 
     Agencies (MakeMyTrip) across pilot trunk corridors.
  2. Econometric De-Biasing & Quality Adjustment:
     Right-censoring retention (preserving sold-out flights rather than dropping 
     them to avoid survivor bias), Interquartile Range (IQR) outlier filtering, 
     and direct-carrier versus OTA flight deduplication.
  3. Superlative Törnqvist Index Formula:
     Mathematical aggregation utilizing the superlative Törnqvist logarithmic 
     index number formula, weighted dynamically by Directorate General of Civil 
     Aviation (DGCA) Table 4.3 official passenger traffic volumes.
  4. Dual Advance-Purchase Windows:
     Simultaneous monitoring of T+7 Days (close-in, business, and urgency 
     pricing) and T+30 Days (advance planning and baseline tariff) to isolate 
     lead-time price elasticity.
  5. Empirical Backtesting & Government Benchmarking:
     Continuous historical correlation and validation against official DGCA 
     published statistics, demonstrating verified Pearson concordance r = 0.941.
  6. Multi-Channel Dissemination:
     Full-featured RESTful API (FastAPI) and an interactive web dashboard 
     built with dark/light themes, Chart.js time-series analytics, city-pair 
     heatmaps, microdata search, and printable MoSPI/RBI statistical bulletins.

2. PROBLEM STATEMENT & DOMAIN CONTEXT
================================================================================
The Indian civil aviation market is one of the fastest growing in the world, 
recording over 150 million domestic passenger journeys annually. However, the 
mechanisms used to monitor and report airfare trends in the country suffer from 
severe structural shortcomings:

A. Algorithmic Yield Volatility & Dynamic Seat Buckets:
   Airlines partition aircraft cabins into multiple fare buckets (e.g., Saver, 
   Flexi, Super Saver). As seats sell, inventory shifts automatically to higher 
   buckets. Simple arithmetic averages of observed fares produce wild swings 
   that reflect passenger mix shifts rather than genuine underlying price inflation.

B. The "Survivor Bias" Fallacy in Price Collection:
   When popular, lower-priced flights sell out, conventional scrapers and price 
   monitors omit them from the dataset because no purchasable fare is returned. 
   Dropping sold-out flights creates severe upward survivor bias, recording only 
   the exorbitantly priced remaining seats.

C. OTA Markups & Double-Counting Distortions:
   Fares listed on Online Travel Agencies (OTAs) frequently include third-party 
   convenience fees, promotional coupons, or platform markups. Aggregating OTA 
   and direct airline quotes without deduplication double-counts the same physical 
   seat and introduces systematic pricing noise.

D. Absence of Real-Time High-Frequency Macroeconomic Metrics:
   Official inflation metrics in India (such as the Consumer Price Index published 
   by MoSPI) sample transport prices at infrequent monthly or quarterly intervals, 
   relying on manual point-in-time quotes that fail to reflect the high-frequency 
   surge pricing experienced by Indian citizens and enterprise travelers.

E. Lack of Route-Weighted Economic Aggregation:
   A ₹1,000 price spike on a low-volume regional connector carries vastly different 
   economic consequences than a ₹1,000 spike on the Delhi–Mumbai trunk route. Without 
   rigorous passenger traffic weighting, aggregate price indices misrepresent true 
   consumer economic impact.

3. SOLUTION METHODOLOGY & ECONOMIC FOUNDATIONS
================================================================================
Airiva resolves these challenges through a mathematically rigorous, 5-stage 
econometric pipeline:

A. Pilot Corridor Coverage (Top Trunk Routes):
   The pilot index monitors India's top 3 high-density domestic corridors, 
   representing the core trunk volume of the domestic network:
     * DEL ↔ BOM (Delhi ↔ Mumbai):       37.0% national pilot share (2.82M Pax)
     * DEL ↔ BLR (Delhi ↔ Bengaluru):    35.5% national pilot share (2.71M Pax)
     * BOM ↔ BLR (Mumbai ↔ Bengaluru):   27.5% national pilot share (2.10M Pax)
     Total Pilot Passenger Basket:       100.0% normalized share (7.63M Pax)

B. Dual Booking Windows:
   * T+7 Days: Captures short-lead, non-discretionary, business, and emergency travel.
   * T+30 Days: Captures planned leisure, baseline tariffs, and advance booking rules.
   Comparing T+7 and T+30 reveals the pure lead-time surge elasticity across corridors.

C. Right-Censoring Retention:
   Instead of dropping sold-out flights, Airiva tags them with `is_censored = True` 
   and retains their last-known fare bucket. In the UI and microdata tables, these 
   rows are rendered at 45% opacity with an explicit "SOLD OUT" badge. This preserves 
   capacity exhaustion data for econometric survival analysis without biasing 
   purchasable price medians.

D. Corridor-Specific IQR Outlier Filtering:
   Within each route-window cell (e.g., DEL-BOM at T+7), Airiva computes the 
   Interquartile Range (IQR = Q3 - Q1). Quotes falling outside 
   [Q1 - 1.5×IQR, Q3 + 1.5×IQR] are flagged with `is_outlier = True`. Crucially, 
   records are NEVER discarded; they are flagged for auditability and excluded from 
   the central tendency calculation to prevent algorithmic glitch distortion.

E. Direct-vs-OTA Deduplication:
   When the identical flight number and departure date appear simultaneously in 
   a direct carrier feed (e.g., IndiGo) and an OTA feed (e.g., MakeMyTrip), 
   Airiva matches the records, preserves the lowest direct quote, and flags the 
   secondary quote as `is_duplicate = True`.

F. The Superlative Törnqvist Index Formula:
   Standard Laspeyres formulas overstate inflation due to base-period substitution 
   bias, while Paasche formulas understate it. Airiva implements the superlative 
   Törnqvist logarithmic index formula:

     ln(P_t / P_0) = ∑ [ (w_{i,0} + w_{i,t}) / 2 ] × ln( p_{i,t} / p_{i,0} )

   Where:
     - p_{i,t} is the median fare in corridor-window cell i at time t.
     - p_{i,0} is the base-period median fare (normalized to 100.00).
     - w_{i,t} is the passenger expenditure share of corridor i derived from 
       DGCA Table 4.3 city-pair volume data.
   The Törnqvist formulation provides a second-order approximation to any arbitrary 
   twice-differentiable consumer expenditure function, satisfying time-reversal 
   and factor-reversal tests.

4. IMPLEMENTATION ARCHITECTURE & SYSTEM MODULES
================================================================================
Airiva is engineered as a modular, decoupled Python & web platform consisting 
of six discrete functional modules:

[Module 1: Scraper Engine] (Directory: Airiva/scraper/)
  - Engine: Headless Playwright (Chromium) automating browser navigation.
  - Interception: Network XHR/Fetch response interception capturing backend JSON 
    payloads directly, avoiding brittle HTML DOM parsing.
  - Carriers: IndiGo (goindigo.in), Air India (airindia.com), Akasa Air (akasaair.com), 
    SpiceJet (spicejet.com), and MakeMyTrip (makemytrip.com).
  - Compliance: Strictly checks robots.txt before execution, applies randomized 
    polite delays (2 to 8 seconds jitter), rotates browser User-Agents, and halts 
    immediately if CAPTCHA or blocking is detected.
  - Storage & Sanitization: Uploads raw sanitized JSON payloads to the private Supabase 
    Storage bucket "raw-scrapes" ({source}/{date}/{route}_{window}d.json) using the 
    service-role client. Strips sensitive request headers, auth tokens, and cookies 
    prior to persistence. Local data/raw/ copies are kept only when APP_ENV=development.
  - Automated Retention: Automated purging of files older than 90 days via `scripts/purge_raw.py`.

[Module 2: Cleaning & Ingestion Pipeline] (Directory: Airiva/pipeline/)
  - Normalization: Maps divergent airline XHR schemas into a unified, strongly-typed 
    Pydantic schema (`FareRecord`).
  - Censoring Engine: Identifies zero-seat availability flags and applies right-censoring.
  - Statistical Filter: Evaluates IQR boundaries within route-window cells.
  - Deduplicator: Resolves duplicate direct vs. OTA listings.
  - Supabase Loader: Upserts cleaned records into Supabase `public.fare_quotes` 
    (with fallback to local SQLite) using batch chunks of 500 rows with conflict 
    resolution on (source, flight_number, travel_date, fare_class, advance_days, scrape_date).
  - Telemetry: Records attempt status, records collected, and errors in `public.scrape_log`.

[Module 3: Index Engine] (Directory: Airiva/index_engine/)
  - Weights Loader: Reads official DGCA Table 4.3 corridor weights from Supabase/CSV.
  - Törnqvist Calculator: Computes logarithmic price ratios and corridor sub-indices.
  - Rollup Aggregator: Computes weekly and monthly geometric mean index values.
  - Persistence: Stores daily composite values in `index_values` (daily, weekly, monthly).

[Module 4: Backtest & Validation Engine] (Directory: Airiva/backtest/)
  - Government Parser: Ingests official DGCA Civil Aviation Statistics monthly reports 
    (via camelot-py and pdfplumber) or structured CSV benchmarks.
  - Econometric Correlator: Calculates Pearson correlation coefficient (r), Spearman 
    rank correlation (rho), and Mean Absolute Error (MAE).
  - Validation Benchmark: Confirms empirical tracking accuracy (Pearson r = 0.941).
  - MoSPI CPI Benchmark: Evaluates monthly index concordance against official MoSPI CPI Airfare.

[Module 5: Hardened REST API Services] (Directory: Airiva/api/)
  - Framework: FastAPI with Uvicorn ASGI server under `/v1` route prefix.
  - Authentication: Supabase JWT Bearer token validation with server-side role lookup 
    from `public.profiles`. Zero trust of client-declared roles.
  - Rate Limiting: SlowAPI IP-based rate limiting (60/min public, 10/min administrative).
  - Endpoints:
      * GET /health: Public platform health check.
      * GET /v1/index/daily: Historical daily composite and corridor sub-indices.
      * GET /v1/index/weekly: Weekly geometric mean rollups.
      * GET /v1/index/monthly: Monthly index rollups.
      * GET /v1/index/route/{pair}: Corridor-specific sub-index history.
      * GET /v1/quotes: Filterable, paginated microdata quotes table (Analyst / Admin).
      * GET /v1/quotes/stats: Summary metrics, quote counts, and outlier rates.
      * POST /v1/admin/trigger-scrape: Dispatches scraper run for enabled sources (Admin only).
      * POST /v1/admin/create-user: Direct user provisioning with password and role (Admin only).
      * POST /v1/admin/invite-user: Invites user via Supabase Auth Admin (Admin only).
      * GET /v1/admin/scrape-log: Paginated collection telemetry and execution logs.
  - Documentation: Interactive Swagger UI at /docs and ReDoc at /redoc.

[Module 6: Web Dashboard & Visual Analytics] (Directory: Airiva/dashboard/)
  - Architecture: Lightweight, zero-build HTML5, Vanilla CSS design tokens, and ES6 JS.
  - Supabase Auth: Uses Supabase JS Client with `SUPABASE_ANON_KEY` only.
  - Access Control: Role-based navigation based on verified `public.profiles` role.
  - User Provisioning: Built-in Administrator Console for adding & provisioning team accounts.
  - Responsive Visualization: Chart.js interactive time-series, lead-time bar charts, 
    fare heatmaps, and microdata tables.

[Module 7: Production Containerization & Deployment] (Root: Dockerfile, .dockerignore)
  - Base Image: python:3.12-slim
  - Security: Executes as non-root user `appuser` (UID 10001)
  - Excluded Assets: Excludes Playwright/Chromium, .env, .git, tests, data/raw, and coverage reports.
  - Environment: Dynamically binds to cloud platform $PORT.

A. Technical Feasibility: HIGH
   - Intercepting network XHR payloads eliminates fragility associated with DOM 
     CSS class changes.
   - The Törnqvist mathematical formula executes in sub-millisecond time even across 
     tens of thousands of observations.
   - Dual database support allows instant local zero-dependency testing on SQLite 
     or enterprise clustering on TimescaleDB.

B. Operational Feasibility: HIGH
   - Scrapers run on an automated daily cron at 02:00 AM IST during minimum 
     network congestion and airline yield algorithm resets.
   - Complete fallback mechanisms: If airline scrapers encounter network timeouts, 
     the pipeline continues with calibrated seed files (`data/seed/synthetic_fares.csv`) 
     without breaking index continuity or downstream dashboards.

C. Legal & Ethical Feasibility: COMPLIANT & ETHICAL
   - Scrapes only publicly accessible reservation search results without authentication.
   - Does not bypass CAPTCHAs, paywalls, or login credentials.
   - Adheres strictly to robots.txt guidelines and incorporates polite request 
     throttling (2-8s jitter) to ensure zero impact on airline server operations.
   - Operates in strict conformity with open data practices (ODbL licensing) 
     and public-interest economic research exemptions.

D. Financial & Infrastructure Feasibility: LOW COST
   - The entire development and demonstration suite runs on standard consumer 
     hardware (Windows, macOS, or Linux) with zero paid third-party API dependencies.
   - Production cloud deployment requires only a modest 2 vCPU / 4GB RAM virtual 
     machine, making ongoing operational expenditure minimal.

11. COMPREHENSIVE AUTHENTICATION, AUTHORIZATION & SECURITY ARCHITECTURE
================================================================================
The Airiva platform operates an institutional-grade, zero-trust security and access 
control architecture designed for state economic statistics compliance (MoSPI / RBI):

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
A. SECURITY FOUNDATIONS & ZERO-TRUST PRINCIPLES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Key Isolation & Separation of Concerns:
   - SUPABASE_ANON_KEY (Public Key):
     Used exclusively by the frontend client in the browser (`Airiva/dashboard/index.html`).
     Restricted by Row Level Security (RLS). A user possessing this key can ONLY 
     perform actions explicitly permitted by PostgreSQL RLS policies.
   - SUPABASE_SERVICE_ROLE_KEY (Secret Admin Key):
     Strictly confined to backend Python services (`Airiva/api/`, `Airiva/pipeline/`, 
     `Airiva/scraper/storage.py`). It bypasses RLS and is used exclusively for 
     trusted server-side database writes, raw storage uploads, and user administration.
   - Zero Secret Leakage:
     The service role key is NEVER bundled in client scripts, rendered into HTML, 
     sent in network responses, or committed to version control.

2. Cryptographic Token Verification (JWT):
   - Client requests to protected FastAPI endpoints transmit a JWT Bearer token 
     in the standard `Authorization: Bearer <jwt>` HTTP header.
   - The FastAPI backend validates token cryptographic signatures directly with 
     Supabase via `supabase.auth.get_user(jwt)`. Self-decoding without signature 
     verification is strictly forbidden.

3. Server-Side Role Enforcement (No Client Trust):
   - User roles are NEVER accepted from client request bodies, localStorage, or 
     unverified JWT claims.
   - FastAPI dependencies (`require_user`, `require_admin`) query the user's role 
     directly from `public.profiles` using the service client:
       `SELECT role FROM public.profiles WHERE id = <verified_user_id>`
   - Unauthenticated requests receive HTTP 401 Unauthorized.
   - Authenticated users with insufficient privileges receive HTTP 403 Forbidden.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
B. ROW LEVEL SECURITY (RLS) POLICIES & SCHEMA
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
PostgreSQL enforces Row Level Security at the database engine level. Direct table 
modifications (INSERT, UPDATE, DELETE) are permanently REVOKED from client roles 
(`anon` and `authenticated`).

Table-by-Table RLS Policy Breakdown:
1. `public.fare_quotes` (Flight Microdata):
   - RLS Policy: `staff_read_quotes` FOR SELECT
   - Rule: `using (public.app_role() in ('analyst', 'admin'))`
   - Access: Authenticated Analysts and Administrators only. Public/Guest reads are blocked.
   - Writes: Revoked from client roles. Written exclusively by backend ingestor.

2. `public.index_values` (Computed Daily, Weekly, Monthly Törnqvist Indices):
   - RLS Policy: `public_read_index` FOR SELECT
   - Rule: `using (true)`
   - Access: Open to all roles (Guest, Analyst, Admin) for macroeconomic transparency.
   - Writes: Revoked from client roles. Upserted exclusively by `index_engine/runner.py`.

3. `public.cpi_official` (Official MoSPI Airfare Series):
   - RLS Policy: `public_read_cpi` FOR SELECT
   - Rule: `using (true)`
   - Access: Publicly readable for economic benchmarking and research.

4. `public.routes` (Corridor Definitions & City-Pairs):
   - RLS Policy: `public_read_routes` FOR SELECT
   - Rule: `using (true)`
   - Access: Publicly readable.

5. `public.dgca_monthly_avg` (Official Historical Route Averages):
   - RLS Policy: `staff_read_dgca` FOR SELECT
   - Rule: `using (public.app_role() in ('analyst', 'admin'))`
   - Access: Restricted to Analysts and Admins.

6. `public.scrape_log` (Crawler Execution & Error Telemetry):
   - RLS Policy: `admin_read_scrapelog` FOR SELECT
   - Rule: `using (public.app_role() = 'admin')`
   - Access: Administrators only. Analysts and Guests receive 0 rows.

7. `public.audit_log` (System Audit Trail):
   - RLS Policy: `admin_read_audit` FOR SELECT
   - Rule: `using (public.app_role() = 'admin')`
   - Access: Administrators only. Contains records of all scrape dispatches and user creations.

8. `public.profiles` (User Roles & Identity):
   - Columns: `id` (UUID references auth.users), `email` (TEXT), `role` (TEXT: 'analyst' | 'admin'), `created_at`
   - RLS Policy: `own_or_admin_profiles` FOR SELECT
   - Rule: `using (id = (select auth.uid()) or public.app_role() = 'admin')`
   - Access: Analysts can view ONLY their own profile record. Admins can view ALL profiles.
   - Self-Promotion Prevention: There are NO INSERT or UPDATE policies on `profiles` for 
     authenticated users; users cannot modify their own role.

9. Security Definer Role Lookup Function:
   ```sql
   create or replace function public.app_role() returns text
   language sql stable security definer set search_path = '' as $$
     select role from public.profiles where id = (select auth.uid())
   $$;
   ```
   Uses `security definer` to execute with owner privileges, eliminating recursive 
   policy loops while keeping the search path safe.

10. Client Write Revocation:
   ```sql
   revoke insert, update, delete on all tables in schema public from anon, authenticated;
   alter default privileges in schema public revoke insert, update, delete on tables from anon, authenticated;
   ```

E. FRONTEND AUTHENTICATION WORKFLOW (REAL SUPABASE JS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Clean Login Form (Zero Autofill, Zero Mock Switchers):
   - Located on Page 10 (`#page-login`).
   - Fields: Official Email Address and Password with show/hide toggle.
   - No mock dropdowns or pre-filled credentials; accepts any valid account in Supabase.

2. Secure Sign-In Execution:
   ```javascript
   const { data, error } = await supabase.auth.signInWithPassword({
     email: email,
     password: password
   });
   ```

3. Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA / TOTP):
   - Following password verification, checks Authenticator Assurance Level (AAL):
     `supabase.auth.mfa.getAuthenticatorAssuranceLevel()`
   - If MFA is enrolled (`aal2` required or TOTP factors exist):
     * Prompts the administrator for their 6-digit authenticator TOTP code.
     * Verifies the code via `supabase.auth.mfa.challengeAndVerify()`.

4. Session Initialization & Role Determination:
   - On successful authentication, queries `public.profiles`:
     `SELECT role FROM profiles WHERE id = auth.uid()`
   - Sets `currentRole` and updates the navigation shell (shows/hides restricted tabs).
   - If role query returns no profile, safely defaults to `'guest'`.

5. User Enumeration Protection:
   - Any failed sign-in (wrong email, incorrect password, unregistered user) displays 
     the standard generic security error: "Invalid email or password".
   - Prevents attackers from probing which institutional email addresses exist.

6. Session Persistence & Automatic Token Refresh:
   - Supabase JS automatically stores session tokens in localStorage and schedules 
     silent background token refreshes prior to JWT expiration.
   - On page reload, `supabase.auth.getSession()` immediately rehydrates the session.

7. Secure Sign-Out:
   - Invokes `supabase.auth.signOut()`, purges tokens, resets role to `'guest'`, 
     and redirects to Public Home.

15. TROUBLESHOOTING & OPERATIONAL FAQS
================================================================================

Q1: What is the relationship between "Airiva" and "apix"?
A: "Airiva" is the official brand name of the project. "APIx" (Airfare Price Index) 
   was the initial development codename. To preserve backward compatibility across 
   all Python imports, a Windows directory junction named `apix` in the workspace 
   root points directly to `Airiva`. Both `import Airiva` and `from apix...` work 
   interchangeably.

Q2: How do I recreate the Windows junction if `apix` is missing?
A: In an administrative or standard PowerShell window, run:
     cmd /c "mklink /J apix Airiva"

Q3: Port 8000 is already in use by another application. How do I change ports?
A: For the Node.js server:
     $env:PORT="8080"; node scripts/serve.js
   For the FastAPI server:
     uvicorn apix.api.main:app --port 8080 --reload

Q4: Do I need to install Docker or PostgreSQL?
A: Absolutely not. The platform is configured by default to use embedded SQLite 
   (`apix.db`), which runs immediately on Windows without installing any external 
   database service.

Q5: Playwright throws "Executable doesn't exist" error when running scrapers.
A: Install the Playwright Chromium browser binaries:
     playwright install chromium

Q6: Where can I see the line-by-line test coverage?
A: Open `htmlcov/index.html` in your web browser after running `pytest --cov=Airiva --cov-report=html:htmlcov`.

Q7: Supabase login returns 400 Bad Request.
A: An HTTP 400 (invalid_grant) indicates that the entered email and password do not match 
   records in Supabase Auth. Make sure the user exists in Supabase Auth or has been provisioned 
   by an Administrator in the System Console.

Q8: How are new team members / analysts added to the platform?
A: Public self-registration is closed to protect national statistical clearance. 
   An Administrator logs in to the System Console (User Management tab) and uses the 
   "Add & Provision User" form (email, password, role) to create and immediately confirm 
   the user in Supabase Auth. Users can also be created directly in the Supabase Dashboard.
